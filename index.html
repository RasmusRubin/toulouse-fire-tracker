WED 29 JUL — 12:00

Status: Small fires in and around Toulouse, contained; Gironde fire now much larger and 15 km from Bordeaux

Haute-Garonne's daily fire-danger actually eased slightly today — Toulouse itself is at orange ("élevé") rather than yesterday's red, though the préfecture's restriction order (no open flames, no spark-risk machinery 12:00–20:00) stays in force regardless. That said, several small fires have flared in the department in the last day: one broke out inside Toulouse itself and was brought under control and extinguished by evening; another was reported near Arnaud-Guilhem, between Saint-Gaudens and Toulouse, with fire crews engaged; scattered brush fires were also logged this week in Escalquens, Merville, Montgaillard, and Avignonet-Lauragais near the A61 — all local and dealt with quickly, not large blazes.

  • Gironde (Bordeaux area): the fire has grown sharply — now around 42,000 hectares burned, roughly 220,000 people evacuated at peak, one of the largest evacuations in French history. Flames reached about 15 km from Bordeaux near Martignas-sur-Jalle; the mayor says evacuating the city itself isn't currently planned. Two firefighters died battling the blaze near Mérignac.
  • A military A400M aircraft, converted into a water bomber carrying 20 tonnes of retardant, has been deployed to reinforce firefighting efforts.
  • Nationally: over 12,500 fire starts and roughly 44,000 hectares burned since January 2026 — already worse than the severe 2022 season.
  • A new heatwave is still forecast to peak around Saturday 1 Aug, which could push Toulouse's risk back up to red.
TUE 28 JUL — 12:00

Status: Elevated risk, no fire in Haute-Garonne, watching the heatwave

Haute-Garonne remains under red fire-danger vigilance but has no active blaze. The department's attention is on a new heatwave arriving from Tuesday, forecast to push temperatures to 37–40°C between Toulouse and the Aquitaine basin, peaking around Saturday 1 Aug.

  • Gironde (Bordeaux area): ~420 km² burned, holding steady overnight; ~4,000 evacuated from Atlantic coast tourist sites (Cap Ferret/Le Porge); A63 highway partly closed.
  • Landes: separate fire now "fixed" (contained), evacuees returning home.
  • Aude/Hérault: ~900 hectares burned near the Spanish border, contained but active earlier in July.
  • Nationally: ~115,000 hectares burned in France so far in 2026; military A400M aircraft now being used as air tankers.
