Gironde fire declared “fixed” — the first time since it began

Two active hotspots remain, at Cap Ferret and south of Lacanau. Nothing significant is burning in Haute-Garonne. Today’s danger level is on the scale above.

Full context

No significant fire is burning in Haute-Garonne. Small ignitions have appeared in and around Toulouse through the week — one inside the city itself, others at Arnaud-Guilhem, Escalquens, Merville and near the A61 — and all were reached and put out quickly by SDIS 31. The préfecture's ban on open flames and spark-risk equipment (12:00–20:00, through 1 September) applies whenever the department is at the Severe level.

The Saumos fire in Gironde burned roughly 42,000 hectares and forced about 224,000 preventive evacuations — one of the largest in French history — but it is over 200 km from Toulouse and the trend has turned. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez declared the fire “fixé” (fixed) on Friday 1 August — a step beyond stabilised, meaning the perimeter is held — with the exception of two hotspots still active at Cap Ferret and south of Lacanau. The A63 reopened on Thursday 30 July and further groups of evacuees were cleared to return home over the weekend.

The weekend heat peak did not translate into a fire-danger spike for Haute-Garonne, which sat at Moderate through Friday and Saturday. The daily level shown on the scale above is now pulled directly from Météo-France every few hours.

THU 6 AUG — 12:00

Gironde shifts to formal reconstruction; small Toulouse-area fire already out; extreme risk flagged further east (Var)

Gironde's recovery has moved from emergency response into organised reconstruction. On Wednesday the préfète installed a steering committee for rebuilding the affected territories, bringing together all 23 affected mayors, the Conseil Départemental, and the Région, and announced an initial €300,000 in state aid. Mayors raised their first priorities: rehousing residents, securing pine trees at risk of falling, managing water in the burn-scarred watershed, and repairing roads. An operational platform, coordinated by a dedicated crisis-management specialist, starts up in Le Porge today to support the cleanup and repair work. Closer to home, a small fire broke out yesterday near Daux, northwest of Toulouse — it was reported, attacked, fixed, and declared out within about two and a half hours, nothing of concern. Further east, well outside our area, the Var and Bouches-du-Rhône are under extreme fire-danger warnings today with most Var forest massifs closed to the public, driven by Mistral winds and heatwave conditions — worth knowing about nationally, but nothing suggests it's moving toward Occitanie.

  • Gironde: reconstruction steering committee installed 5 August with the 23 affected mayors, the Conseil Départemental, and the Région; €300,000 in initial state aid announced.
  • Mayors' early priorities: rehousing, securing at-risk pines, watershed water management, road repairs.
  • An operational recovery platform, run by a crisis-management coordinator, starts in Le Porge today, 6 August, to support cleanup and rebuilding.
  • Daux (Haute-Garonne, ~15 km NW of Toulouse): small fire reported 11:10 yesterday, fixed by 12:30, declared out by 13:46. No ongoing concern.
  • Var / Bouches-du-Rhône (not Haute-Garonne): extreme fire-danger warnings today; 8 of the Var's 9 forest massifs closed to the public amid Mistral winds and a heatwave.
  • Haute-Garonne: today's danger level is confirmed Moderate via the automated pipeline — check the scale above rather than this entry.
TUE 4 AUG — 12:00

Gironde fire fully reopened: last two communes cleared; vigilance downgraded red → orange

Monday brought the biggest step yet in Gironde's recovery. The préfecture authorised return to the final two evacuated communes, Le Porge and Lège-Cap-Ferret — meaning every community evacuated since the fire began on 22 July has now been cleared to go home. Le Porge's return was delayed slightly for a demining sweep after WWII-era ordnance turned up during firefighting; crews recovered over 400 shells before letting residents back in that evening. Several untouched campsites in Lège-Cap-Ferret reopened the same night, and a dedicated victim-support office opened in Le Porge to help with paperwork and claims. Effective today, the département has formally lowered its fire-danger vigilance level from red to orange, reopening some activity in forest-adjacent areas — though officials are clear the fire is “fixé,” not “éteint,” and prevention restrictions remain. Haute-Garonne itself continues to have no active fire activity.

  • Return authorised as of Monday 3 Aug for Le Porge and Lège-Cap-Ferret — the last two communes still under evacuation. All evacuated communities are now cleared.
  • Le Porge: a demining operation recovered 400+ WWII-era shells uncovered during firefighting, ahead of residents returning.
  • Several untouched Lège-Cap-Ferret campsites reopened Monday evening; a Maison France Services Victimes opened in Le Porge for residents' claims and paperwork.
  • The fire remains “fixé” but not “éteint” — crews are still extinguishing smouldering hotspots on the coastal fringe; officials caution against complacency.
  • Gironde's fire-danger vigilance level drops from red to orange (3/5) effective today, 4 August, reopening some forest-adjacent activity while prevention restrictions remain in force.
  • Haute-Garonne: no active fires reported today. Check the scale above for today's confirmed danger level — it pulls directly from Météo-France, not this entry.
MON 3 AUG — 12:00

Gironde recovery moving forward; small Aude fire already out; nothing in Haute-Garonne

The Gironde fire keeps trending the right way. Saturday evening's préfecture briefing cleared four more communes for return — Marcheprime, Arès, Saumos and Le Temple — plus two neighbourhoods of Audenge, bringing the total authorised home to around 208,000 people since the fire began. Only Lège-Cap-Ferret and Le Porge remain off-limits, where crews are still watching hotspots. Today the department's forestry sector meets to start planning timber-industry support and longer-term forest rehabilitation — itself a sign the emergency phase is closing. Separately, a new fire broke out in the Aude (east of here, a different department) but was already declared fixed by Saturday. Haute-Garonne itself has had no significant fire activity.

  • Saumos fire (Gironde): ~42,000 hectares burned, 240 km of fire edge total — one of the largest evacuations in French history. Declared “fixé” on 1 August; two hotspots remain active (Cap Ferret, south of Lacanau).
  • As of the Saturday 1 Aug 20:00 briefing: ~208,000 people authorised to return since the fire began. Lège-Cap-Ferret and Le Porge are not yet cleared.
  • Firefighter toll: 330+ injured since the fire began. No deaths or injuries reported among the public.
  • Monday 3 August: Gironde's forestry companies convene to plan support for the timber industry and forest rehabilitation — the response is shifting from emergency to recovery.
  • New fire in the Aude (not Haute-Garonne): roughly 100 hectares burned, ~100 people evacuated, a road and a motorway briefly cut. Declared fixed as of Saturday (France 3 Régions, reported Sunday 2 Aug).
  • Haute-Garonne: nothing burning. Today's danger level is Moderate — check the scale above, which pulls directly from Météo-France rather than this entry.
SUN 2 AUG — 12:00

Gironde fire now “fixed” apart from two hotspots; quiet week ahead for Haute-Garonne

The Gironde fire crossed a real threshold on Friday: Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez declared it “fixé” — fixed — which in French fire-service terms is a step beyond “stabilised” and means the perimeter is being held rather than merely not advancing. Two hotspots are still active, at Cap Ferret and south of Lacanau, so it is not finished, but the emergency phase is over and evacuees have continued returning home through the weekend. Haute-Garonne has had no significant fire at any point this week.

  • Saumos fire (Gironde): ~42,000 hectares, ~220,000 preventive evacuations at peak. Declared fixed on 1 August, with active hotspots remaining at Cap Ferret and south of Lacanau.
  • Nationally: more than 119,000 hectares burned across France so far in 2026 — described by authorities as an exceptional season.
  • Haute-Garonne: nothing burning. The daily danger level on the scale above now comes straight from Météo-France and refreshes through the day, so it is the thing to check rather than this entry.

FRI 31 JUL — 12:00

Gironde fire stabilised, A63 reopened, 144,000 allowed home; nothing significant burning in Haute-Garonne

The direction of travel has changed. The Gironde préfecture's Thursday afternoon situation report describes the Saumos fire as stabilised, with evacuees returning in stages and the motorway open again. It is not out — flare-ups are still being logged daily — but it is no longer growing. Haute-Garonne itself has had no significant fire; the small starts around Toulouse this week were all contained the same day.

  • Saumos fire (Gironde): ~42,000 hectares total. Stabilised, under constant surveillance. 14 flare-ups recorded Wednesday and three pockets still active that evening.
  • 144,000 people now authorised to return home, following Eysines, Mérignac and Le Haillan from Tuesday. Remaining returns need prefectural authorisation.
  • A63 motorway reopened in both directions from 14:00 on Thursday 30 July.
  • Official toll: 240 homes destroyed and 231 firefighters injured since the fire began. No deaths among the public; two firefighters died earlier near Mérignac.
  • 26 people have been arrested for endangering life — discarded cigarettes, burning off, and dangerous activity in the forest.
  • Météo-France bulletin issued Thursday 30 July at 17:00: Haute-Garonne at Moderate (yellow) for both Friday 31 July and Saturday 1 August. Read directly off the official map.
  • Nationally: 9 departments at High on Friday and 7 on Saturday — none at Severe on either day. The remaining orange sits along the Mediterranean (Aude, Hérault, Gard), not around Toulouse.
  • The Saturday heat peak has not translated into a fire-danger spike for Haute-Garonne. Neighbouring Hautes-Pyrénées actually drops to Low on Saturday.
WED 29 JUL — 12:00 (rev. afternoon)

Small fires in and around Toulouse, contained; Gironde fire now much larger and 15 km from Bordeaux

Local ignitions this week have been fast and small — quickly reached and put out by SDIS 31. The regional picture is dominated by the Gironde fire, which is now large enough to be shaping national response and drawing in outside resources.

  • Gironde: ~42,000 hectares burned, ~220,000 evacuated at peak — one of the largest evacuations in French history. Two firefighters died near Mérignac.
  • Fresh evacuations at Marcheprime and parts of Cestas after the Saumos fire flared up; the Gironde front was described as "très défavorable" at this point.
  • Some Gironde evacuees are being housed at the MEETT centre on the edge of Toulouse — the relief effort reaching here, not the fire.
  • Military A400M water bomber deployed, 20 tonnes of retardant per pass.
  • Nationally: 12,500+ fire starts, ~44,000 hectares burned since January 2026.
